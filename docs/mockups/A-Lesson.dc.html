The Jab

Your lead hand's straight punch. It measures distance, sets up every combination and keeps your opponent honest.

Beginner|8 min lesson|No equipment|Needs: Stance, Guard

[Coach name] · Boxing coach · Reviewed by [Reviewer name]

Step by step

  1. 1

    Set your stance

    Feet shoulder-width apart, lead foot forward, knees soft. Hands at your cheeks, elbows in, chin tucked.

  2. 2

    Extend straight from the guard

    Push the lead hand out on a straight line. No wind-up: the punch starts where your hand already is.

  3. 3

    Turn the fist over

    Rotate so the palm faces down as the arm straightens. Your lead shoulder rises to protect your chin.

  4. 4

    Snap it back on the same line

    Return to your cheek as fast as you threw it. The rear hand never leaves your face.

Common mistakes

Dropping the rear hand

Fix: touch your rear glove to your cheek on every rep.

Pulling back to load

Fix: throw from where the hand rests. A wind-up shows the punch early.

Leaning over the front foot

Fix: keep your head behind your lead knee.

Safety

Warm up your shoulders first. Do not snap the elbow to a hard lockout, especially in the air. Wrap your hands before bag work.

Practice this

  • Stationary jab3 × 2 min
  • Step and jab3 × 2 min
  • Jab on the heavy bag4 × 2 min
  • Jab to target3 × 2 min

Ask about the jab

You asked

Why does my jab feel slow?

AI explanation

Most slow jabs start with a wind-up. Throw from the guard, relax the shoulder, and focus on the snap back, not the reach.
